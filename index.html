Vega Deflection Market Making

The idea

A deflection market maker holds no opinion on what volatility should be. We quote around the mid, collect edge from customers, and let their trades push our fair value around. Every fill deflects our volatility fair in the direction the customer traded, for two reasons: an aggressor is a market signal, and we will give up a little edge to avoid carrying a large absolute vega position.

The board is a single expiry with seven strikes, the fourth at the money. Black–Scholes gives each call and put a theoretical value and each strike a vega, written ν: the change in option price for a one point (1%) move in volatility. There is no skew, so the call and put on a strike share one ν.

Input 1: how much vega we show for one ν of edge

The first input, Q, is the position vega per fill per instrument vega. I call it the quote depth. It is the amount of vega we are willing to trade when the edge we charge equals one ν of the instrument. Edge and size scale together: twice the vega costs twice the edge.

edge in vegas = lots × ν / Q     edge in price = lots × ν² / Q

Futures are at 100. The 100 calls are worth 10.00 with ν = 0.50, and Q = 15.

  • 30 lots carry 30 × 0.50 = 15 vega. That is one ν of edge, so the market is 9.50 at 10.50, 30 up.
  • 60 lots carry 30 vega. That is two ν of edge, so the market is 9.00 at 11.00, 60 up.

Input 2: how much vega moves our vol by one point

The second input, D, is the position vega per vol point bump per instrument vega. I call it the deflection depth. When a customer takes D vega from us, we move our volatility fair by one full point: up if they bought vega, down if they sold it. Every fair on the board then moves by its own ν times that bump.

Δσ = ± lots × ν / D     new fair = old fair + Δσ × ν

With D = 25, suppose we are lifted on the 30 lot offer at 10.50. The customer bought 15 vega, so our vol goes up 15 / 25 = 0.60 points. The call's fair becomes 10.00 + 0.60 × 0.50 = 10.30 and the next one-ν market is 9.80 at 10.80, 30 up.

Had we been lifted on the 60 lot offer at 11.00 instead, the customer would hold 30 vega, our vol would rise 30 / 25 = 1.20 points and the fair would be 10.00 + 1.20 × 0.50 = 10.60.

Why D must be larger than Q

A fill at k vegas of edge is a position of k × Q vega, which moves our fair k × Q / D vegas toward the trade price. The edge we keep against our own new fair is

retained edge = k × (1 − Q / D) vegas

In the example we sold at 10.50 and our new fair is 10.30, so we keep 0.20, which is 0.4 ν = 1 − 15/25. If D were not larger than Q, the fair would jump through the trade price and we would regret every trade the moment it printed.

Linear or quadratic sizing

Everything above uses linear sizing: the vega we show grows in proportion to the edge we charge. Write k = edge / ν for the edge measured in vegas.

linear:   lots = Q × k / ν     edge in price = lots × ν² / Q

Practise also offers quadratic sizing, where the position vega we take grows with the square of the edge in vegas. Doubling the edge buys four times the size. At one ν of edge both modes agree: Q vega.

quadratic:   position vega = Q × k²     lots = Q × k² / ν     edge in price = ν × √(lots × ν / Q)

With Q = 5, ν = 0.50 and 1.50 of edge, k = 3, so we take 5 × 3² = 45 vega, which is 45 / 0.50 = 90 lots. Asked for a market in 40 lots of the same option, the position is 20 vega and the edge is 0.50 × √(20 / 5) = 1.00 a side. The vol bump after a fill is unchanged in both modes: Δσ = ± lots × ν / D.

Spreads

A spread is priced as one instrument. Its fair is the signed sum of its legs' fairs and its ν is the signed sum of its legs' vegas. Then the same two formulas apply.

InstrumentLegs (what the buyer gets)Net ν
Straddle+1 call, +1 put, same strikelarge, positive
Strangle+1 lower put, +1 higher calllarge, positive
Call spread+1 lower call, −1 higher callsmall, either sign
Put spread+1 higher put, −1 lower putsmall, either sign
Butterfly+1, −2, +1 at equal spacingusually negative near the money
Condor+1, −1, −1, +1usually negative near the money
Ladder+1, −1, −1 moving away from the first strikeusually negative

Watch the sign. A customer who buys an instrument with negative ν has sold vega, so our vol goes down. The formula handles it: Δσ = +lots × ν / D when they buy and −lots × ν / D when they sell, with ν carrying its own sign. Edge uses ν², so it is always positive.

The OEV view

Option equivalent vega rescales every option's vega by the vega of the ATM straddle, so the ATM straddle is 1.00 and the ATM call and put are 0.50 each. The OEV view lists each strike's OEV and one true number, the ATM straddle's ν. To recover a vega, multiply.

OEV = ν / νATM straddle     ν = OEV × νATM straddle

With one expiry and one volatility, gamma and vega are proportional across strikes, so dividing gammas by the ATM straddle's gamma gives the same OEV numbers.

The market has a story

Each game, the market's vol follows a path. It might drift steadily up all session, or rally hard and then give some back near the end. It turns at most once, and the pace is not constant. You never see the path while you play. You see it in who trades with you: when the market is above your fair, customers lift your offers more often than they hit your bids, and the other way round. At the end of the game a chart shows the market's path against the vol you were carrying.

Order implied volatility mode

This mode has no deflection depth. Your vol fair is not moved by a formula. It is set by what large customers show you.

  • On your first or second market, a big customer arrives. If that market scored, they trade on one side of it; if it scored 0, they don't trade. Either way they leave large orders 0.5 to 1.5 vol points above the starting vol (bids) or below it (offers), in the legs of that instrument and the at-the-money call and put. Those orders appear on the board next to the starting values, for example (x500)5.25 – for a bid or – 5.60(x500) for an offer, with the size written small below the line.
  • Take the latest big order's vol as your new reference and rebase your fair to just above a big bid, or just below a big offer. Treat every option as tightly correlated, so that vol applies across the board, other strikes and the other expiry included.
  • A sensible market then has the right width for your Q, centred so that the big order is your hedge. With a big bid at price P in your instrument's terms, your bid must be at or below P, so a customer who sells to you can be passed straight on to the big bidder, and your fair must be at or above P. With a big offer it is the mirror image.
  • Small customers will then sell to you (under a big bid) or buy from you (under a big offer). These are the easy trades: each comes with a hedge.
  • Once the market's story has moved at least half a vol point past the latest big order, another big customer trades the same way through your next market and leaves fresh large orders 0.5 to 1.5 points further on, and you rebase again. If the story turns, the old orders are pulled and a big order appears on the other side.
  • Before any big order your reference is the starting implied vol.
  • Each market allows three attempts. If it is still not sensible after the third, you are shown why along with a sensible market. A customer trades on it, on the side that hurts, unless it was simply too wide, which gives nobody anything to pick off.

There is no single right answer, so a sensible market scores 3 points on the first try, 2 on the second and 1 on the third. Your PnL is shown live, marked at the vol of the latest big order.

Two expiries

Practise can deal two expiries on the same future, both starting at the same implied vol. Each board has its own strikes and vegas, and instruments name their expiry in days. There is one vol fair for both: a fill in either expiry moves it by lots × ν / D, with the ν of the expiry that traded.

How Practise scores you

  • You are given the board once, at the starting implied volatility. It never updates. Tracking your vol fair and your fairs is your job.
  • A market step names an instrument and a size. Enter the bid and offer: fair ± edge, to the nearest 0.01 tick, where edge = lots × ν² / Q (linear) or ν × √(lots × ν / Q) (quadratic).
  • A sizing step has a customer offering to trade at a price, for example “sell the 90/110 strangle at 3.00”. Enter how many lots you will trade. If the price gives you no edge against your own fair, the answer is 0. Otherwise the answer is the most lots your edge pays for, rounded down to whole lots: edge × Q / ν² (linear) or Q × (edge / ν)² / ν (quadratic). Sizing answers are not forced onto the 0.05 vol grid, because the customer's price is a 0.01 tick.
  • A trade step tells you the customer hit or lifted your full size. Enter your new vol fair, to 0.01 of a vol point. Sometimes the customer passes and nothing changes.
  • A vol check step asks for your current vol fair. The first step of every game is one.
  • Each step allows three attempts. An answer that lands in any accuracy band below ends the step and scores (3, 2 or 1 points for the first, second or third attempt) × (its accuracy multiplier), so a step is worth up to 9.
  • Accuracy is judged by vega. Your answer is worked backwards to the ν that would make it exactly right. If that ν is within 10% of the true ν the multiplier is 3×, within 20% it is 2×, within 30% it is 1×. For example, with Q = 15 and the 100 calls at 10.00 with ν = 0.50, the 30 lot market is 9.50 at 10.50. Making it 9.40 at 10.60 instead implies ν = √(0.60 × 15 / 30) = √0.30 = 0.548, which is within 10%, so it scores 3×. In quadratic sizing the same width implies a different ν, judged the same way.
  • Where ν enters twice, both uses are checked. A market's width implies one ν and its centre implies another (fair = board value + vol move × ν), and the worse of the two sets the multiplier. If your vol fair has not moved from the board's, the centre must be right to the tick. A sizing answer implies ν through the lots formula, and a new vol fair implies ν through Δσ = lots × ν / D. A vol check with no trade has no ν in it, so it scores 3× exact, 2× within 0.10 and 1× within 0.30.
  • An answer outside every band uses up an attempt. After three misses, or after an answer that scores but is not exact, you are shown the exact answer and your current vol fair, which the game resets to the exact value. Close the box and it is gone.
  • The clock allows 30 seconds per step and keeps running while a box is open. If it runs out, the game ends there. If you finish early, every full 10 seconds left adds 3 points.
  • Sizes are always between 0.5 ν and 2 ν of edge, and in whole lots that move your vol by a multiple of 0.05 points. Implied vol runs from 15% to 80%. Vegas on the board are rounded to the nearest 0.05. In OEV view the OEVs are rounded to 0.01 from the true vegas and the ATM straddle ν is rounded to a multiple of 0.05, so sizes move your vol by a multiple of 0.01 instead.
  • Conventions: fairs move linearly, fair = board value + (vol fair − starting vol) × ν. Use the board's rounded values and vegas exactly as shown. Vol fair is carried at two decimals after each trade. In OEV view, ν = OEV × ATM straddle ν using the displayed numbers.
